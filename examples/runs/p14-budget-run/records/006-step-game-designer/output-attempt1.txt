## Rulings of step 05 (govern over DR2, DR4–DR8 and the "iff accepted" wording where they differ)

Source: D-028. No rule or number changes.

**Ready time.** `Tready` is the first frame on which both the dash and the recharge timer are at or below 0. - `Tready − Tuse` is at least 0.85 s (never early) and at most 0.85 s plus 2 frames: 0.917 s at 30 FPS, 0.883 s at 60 FPS.
- **0.9 s at 30 FPS passes.** It is 0.05 s over, which is within 2 frames. It is not a defect for this Goal.

# D-028: Dash ready time is 0.85 s plus up to 2 frames; the header ignores hit-stun

## Decision

1. Ready time is dash start + 0.85 s, never earlier, at most 2 frames later (0.917 s at 30 FPS, 0.883 s at 60 FPS). 0.9 s at 30 FPS passes. At 30 FPS it must also stay at or below 0.9 s to keep the DR8 probe results.
2. The cooldown of 0.6 s starts when the dash ends (combat.md reworded).
3. Header, `dash ready` line and `dash_ready_in` follow the dash timers only. In the 0.3 s hit-stun the header may say `dash: ready` while every input, dash included, is refused with `(nothing happens)`. The manual says so.
4. Build number stays 0.6. No existing test needs updating for it.

Limits: no dash number or rule changes. The 2-frame bound is not a license to add delay; the DR8 table still governs.

## Rationale

Two independent frame roundings (dash end, recharge end) explain a delay of 1 to 2 frames without a defect. The Goal is information, not feel. Stun refuses all inputs and is already shown by the hit message, so a new header state adds cost and no information.

## Alternatives considered

- Header shows not ready or a stun state in hit-stun: truthful to the letter, but a new state and text for a 0.3 s case.
- Require the first frame at or after 0.85 s (0.867 s at 30 FPS): stricter; would force Build to change timer stepping, which touches the dash numbers' behavior.
- Bump the build number: not needed, no rule changed, banner tests would need edits.

## Revisit when

A playtest says the header's `ready` in hit-stun misleads, or QA finds a ready time beyond the bound.
