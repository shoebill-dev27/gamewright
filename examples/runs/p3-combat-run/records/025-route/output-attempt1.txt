# 23: studio → qa — Verify the dash/hit invulnerability fix, including dash and attack presses during hit-stun (attempt 3)

## Task

Verify the dash/hit invulnerability fix against the acceptance criteria in `05-qa-to-studio.md`, at 30 and 60 FPS. Give `pass` or `fail`. The new part to cover is criteria 2 and 5: a dash or attack pressed during the 0.3 s hit-stun (D-003).

## Context

Goal G-001 (combat monotony). Your two earlier attempts returned `question`. The first had no build in the packet. The second could not observe what a dash or attack press during hit-stun does, because the build description said only "behaves as it did in 0.3". The build has since been changed to make that behavior explicit. The change is stated below, and `sim/build-0.4.md` is the updated description of it. Take `sim/build-0.4.md` as the latest build and `sim/build-0.3.md` as the baseline. The file name says 0.4 but its heading says "0.3.1". Treat them as one build and note the naming mismatch in your report. It is a labeling issue, not a verification matter.

## Findings

Change in the build (a statement of what was changed and how to exercise it, not a verdict):

- During hit-stun, dash and attack presses are discarded. Nothing is stored, and nothing fires when hit-stun ends.
- Hit-stun is 0.3 s, measured on the accumulated game clock (not a frame count). It should behave the same at 30 and 60 FPS.
- A press while the player is in hit-stun does not start an action. It does not enter the "attack queued during a dash" slot, and it does not enter any other buffer.
- When a hit lands, any pending dash or attack request is cleared.
- Presses are edge-triggered. A button held through the end of hit-stun does not fire when hit-stun ends. A new press afterwards is needed.
- A press at or after the 0.3 s edge is accepted normally. A press exactly on the edge may differ by one frame between 30 and 60 FPS.
- Unchanged: dash distance, duration (0.25 s) and the 0.6 s cooldown; hit invulnerability to 0.8 s; the attack queued during a dash still fires at the dash's end; enemies, rooms and attacks.

Exercise (at 30 FPS and 60 FPS):

1. Take a hit. Press dash at about 0.05, 0.15 and 0.28 s after the hit. Expect that no dash starts then, none starts at 0.3 s, and the dash cooldown does not start.
2. Repeat with attack. Expect no swipe, either then or at 0.3 s.
3. Repeat 1 and 2 with the button held through the end of hit-stun. Expect nothing to fire at 0.3 s, and a fresh press afterwards to work.
4. Press dash and attack as fresh presses just after 0.3 s. Expect both to work, with the dash fully protected (criteria 1 and 2).
5. Run the dash-delay matrix from your earlier report (about 0.3 to 0.85 s), including the points just before, at and after 0.8 s.
6. Re-check criteria 1–6 from `05-qa-to-studio.md` as before. For criterion 6, look at the frame right after a dash ends while a damage source is still in contact.

Criteria most affected: 2 (dash pressed during hit-stun) and 5 (hit-stun lasts 0.3 s with no input). Report all six.

## Decisions already made

- D-001: one attack button plus dash; no new input.
- D-003: input during hit-stun is ignored, not buffered. Hit and dash invulnerability are independent timers.

## Constraints

- Verify from the criteria, D-003 and the build. Do not change the spec or the build.
- Both 30 and 60 FPS must be covered. Give the result per frame rate where they differ.
- Flicker and sound cues: none are described for invulnerability. Record them as "none described, not checked".
- If a part is still unobservable, say exactly which statement is missing from the build description. Do not infer behavior from intent.

## Open questions

None for you to route. Report whether the hit-stun press behavior is observable in the build description and, if so, whether it matches D-003.

## Expected output

A verification report with `pass` or `fail` per criterion and overall, addressed to `studio`. A fail gives the criterion, the case and the observed behavior. The report also records the build-file naming mismatch.

# 24: studio → art-director — Realign the Lunge Grub committed-stage visual to the corrected dash window; decide hit feedback for flinch-immune enemies

## Task

Update `design/g001-telegraph-visuals.md` so the Lunge Grub's committed-stage visual matches the corrected dash window in `design/g001-combat-escalation.md` (section 4.1–4.6). Restate acceptance criterion A5 accordingly. Also decide the hit feedback for enemies that are flinch-immune.

## Context

Goal G-001, Phase 2 (enemy behaviors that demand movement and the dash, per D-002). You wrote the telegraph visuals in handoff 17. The Game Designer then adjusted the Phase 2 spec (handoff 22, D-005) and corrected the dash window. A dash covers a lunge hit only if it starts within about 0.25 s before contact and not after contact. A dash started at the lunge start or later (up to contact) always covers it. The earlier "committed stage solid at 0.35 s means dash now" cue is therefore wrong: a dash started at 0.35 s ends at 0.60 s and covers nothing. No lunge or dash timing changed.

## Findings

From the Designer (handoff 22, spec 4.6):

- The committed stage should start at **0.45 s** and run to lunge start at 0.60 s. 0.45 s is provisional: it depends on distance and hitbox sizes, which could not be obtained from the code notes (see Open questions).
- The flash at lunge start is part of the cue.
- A5 is restated: a dash at lunge start always avoids the hit. A dash at the start of the committed stage avoids it only for close contact.
- Lunge Grub is flinch-immune in Wind-up, Lunge and Recovery. Fan Spitter is flinch-immune in all states. A swipe still damages them but never interrupts, delays or shortens anything. The player must still be able to tell that a swipe landed even though the enemy does not react. The Designer did not ask for this in handoff 19.
- Fan Spitter visuals stand as written. Phase 1 visuals are untouched.

## Decisions already made

- D-004: time-parametrized, sub-stepped lunge hit check; Fan Spitter; gameplay numbers are data, not code constants.
- D-005: Phase 2 enemies are flinch-immune during their actions; Lunge Grub damages only through the lunge; the dash window is defined relative to contact.

## Constraints

- No gameplay timing change. If a visual cannot work with the spec's timing, hand back to `game-designer` with the reason and do not change timing yourself.
- Threats stay saturated orange/red and readable in the dark (pillar 3). Keep consistent with the art direction.
- The committed-stage start may need to change again once real values are known. Express it as a tunable value that follows the spec.
- Small team: reuse existing effects where possible.
- Visuals only. No new input.

## Open questions

- Hit feedback for flinch-immune enemies (hit flash, spark, sound or similar), within the existing effect budget.
- Whether the realigned committed-stage cue stays readable at 30 FPS, where 0.45 s to 0.60 s is about 5 frames.
- Hitbox sizes, speeds, hearts and room size are "not in the code notes" (handoff 20). The Designer's "confirm after the report" values stay provisional. Do not wait for them.

## Expected output

An updated `design/g001-telegraph-visuals.md` with the realigned committed stage, the restated A5 and the flinch-immune hit-feedback rule. Then a handoff addressed to `studio` that states what changed, whether the cue needs further values, and anything the Designer must decide.

## Load

- Skills: studio/skills/write-design-spec.md
- Project: design/g001-telegraph-visuals.md; design/g001-combat-escalation.md (section 4 and criteria 2, 4, 5, 7–9); project.md#art-direction; project.md#vision
- Decisions: decisions/D-004-phase-2-technical-approach.md; decisions/D-005-phase-2-flinch-contact-and-dash-window.md

# G-001: Improve monotonous combat

## Owner request

> 戦闘が単調なので改善したい

## Interpretation

Success condition (qualitative): a player from the project's player profile experiences combat in the current build as varied and engaging rather than repetitive. Playing a run involves meaningful moment-to-moment variation in how the player positions, times and responds to enemies. Numeric thresholds belong to Design; the final verdict belongs to Review.

Constraints that shape any solution:
- Pillar 1 and D-001: combat stays one-button (attack + dash). No new input buttons without Owner approval. Variety must come from positioning, timing and enemy behavior.
- Readable threats (pillar 3) and 30/60 FPS correctness must be preserved.
- Small team: prefer reusing existing systems.

Out of scope: new input buttons or a larger player kit, changes to run length, new biomes or rooms unrelated to combat variety, and non-combat systems.

Not an immediate escalation: the Goal does not conflict with the vision or D-001. It must be solved inside them. If Diagnose finds that the only viable fixes need a new button or change a pillar, that triggers an Owner escalation then.

## Unknowns

Entry questions from `development-loop.md`:
- Observe: has the problem been observed first-hand? No. It is an experience problem (the game may work as specified but feel wrong), so `playtester` observes. The Owner's wording is not given to the Playtester, to avoid confirming a hypothesis.
- Diagnose: is the cause understood? No. What makes the combat feel monotonous is unknown (enemy behavior, kit depth, room design, pacing). Stage created.
- Decide: is there a real choice of direction, priority or scope? Yes. The diagnosis (handoff 04) offered three options (A: enemy behaviors, B: encounter design and escalation, C: rule rewards). Answered by D-002 (B first, then A; C in reserve).
- Design: is the intended behavior specified well enough to build? Not for the chosen direction. Done for Phase 1 and for Phase 2 (handoff 11, `design/g001-combat-escalation.md`, adjusted in handoff 22 and D-005). The Phase 2 telegraph visuals need realignment to the corrected dash window (step 5d).
- Plan: needed for Phase 2 (feasibility and cost). Answered in handoff 16 and D-004: feasible and cheap. Phase 1 needs no separate Plan (handoff 16).
- Build: the dash-damage defect fix (done, handoff 08), the hit-stun input made observable (done, handoff 20), then Phase 1 and Phase 2 of the chosen direction.
- Verify: always after Build.
- Play: the Goal is about how the game feels, so a Playtester re-evaluates after each phase (fresh Handoff composed by the Studio without implementation history). It should record real-time run length and per-room clear time.
- Review: experience Goal, so the Game Director judges the outcome. Planned.
- Observe (defect): the Playtester saw a possible dash-damage defect. QA confirmed it against `design/combat.md` (handoff 05). Intended behavior is already specified, so the fix needed no Design or Plan.

Skipped: none.
