## Findings

QA items for the Designer (none fails a criterion):

1. **Spec 3.3 wording.** The build states, and spec 3.2a/3.3 only imply: (a) same-start enemies are handled in a fixed order, and a ring started by an earlier one counts as occupying its point when the next is chosen; (b) enemies that have not yet started their ring do not count as occupancy; (c) the point is read on the first logic frame at or after the scheduled start. Decide whether to add these to 3.3 word for word, and whether "table order" is the intended fixed order (the build says "a fixed order" only).
2. **Spitter position, rooms 1–2.** Spec 3.4 puts the rooms 1–2 Spitter at E2 (mid-height), with E1 and E3 as alternatives. The build says generally that Spitters appear about 1.5 tiles along the wall from a corner. Confirm which statement is intended for rooms 1–2 and for rooms 3–5, and whether the room 3 Spitter corner (the build says "north edge near a corner") must be named in the spec.
3. **Overlap examples.** The build names room 3 wave 2 and room 5 wave 1 (west edge) only. Room 4 wave 1 also has two Grubs on the west edge at once. Say whether the spec needs the room 4 case named.
4. **Sampling caveat.** The build names only a fast move across the 5-tile boundary; the spec's margin rule also covers the 1.5-tile occupancy threshold. Confirm that the margin rule is the whole rule.
5. **Appearance log basis.** The criterion 4 no-tolerance check needs the scheduled appearance time as well as the scheduled shot time. State what the log must carry, if you hold that rule; wording of the build's log description would go to Build.
6. Carried unchanged for the playtest (spec 3.8 and 6a): the existing values (speeds, hearts, room size, hitboxes) are not available from the build; `SPAWN_FALLBACK` counts per room, arrival gaps of "simul." Grubs and real-time run length must be recorded by the playtest.

Studio note: a pending question for you, not an escalation. If the playtest is also a description with no counted attempts, criteria 1 and 2 stay "not judged". Say in your answer whether the record sheet is written so a tester can give counts where they exist and says "not available" otherwise, so Review is never offered an uncounted pass.

# G-001: Improve monotonous combat

## Owner request

> 戦闘が単調なので改善したい

## Interpretation

Success condition (qualitative): a player from the project's player profile experiences combat in the current build as varied and engaging rather than repetitive. Playing a run involves meaningful moment-to-moment variation in how the player positions, times and responds to enemies. Numeric thresholds belong to Design; the final verdict belongs to Review.

Constraints that shape any solution:
- Pillar 1 and D-001: combat stays one-button (attack + dash). No new input buttons without Owner approval. Variety must come from positioning, timing and enemy behavior.
- Readable threats (pillar 3) and 30/60 FPS correctness must be preserved.
- Small team: prefer reusing existing systems.

Out of scope: new input buttons or a larger player kit, changes to run length, new biomes or rooms unrelated to combat variety, and non-combat systems.

Not an immediate escalation: the Goal does not conflict with the vision or D-001. It must be solved inside them. If Diagnose finds that the only viable fixes need a new button or change a pillar, that triggers an Owner escalation then.

## Unknowns

Entry questions from `development-loop.md`:
- Observe: has the problem been observed first-hand? No. It is an experience problem (the game may work as specified but feel wrong), so `playtester` observes. The Owner's wording is not given to the Playtester, to avoid confirming a hypothesis.
- Diagnose: is the cause understood? No. What makes the combat feel monotonous is unknown (enemy behavior, kit depth, room design, pacing). Stage created.
- Decide: is there a real choice of direction, priority or scope? Yes. The diagnosis (handoff 04) offered three options (A: enemy behaviors, B: encounter design and escalation, C: rule rewards). Answered by D-002 (B first, then A; C in reserve).
- Design: is the intended behavior specified well enough to build? Not for the chosen direction. Done for Phase 1 and for Phase 2 (handoff 11, `design/g001-combat-escalation.md`, adjusted in handoff 22 and D-005). The Phase 2 telegraph visuals were realigned to the corrected dash window in step 5d (handoff 25, D-006, `design/g001-telegraph-visuals.md`). One data value (`committed_start`, provisional 0.45 s) is still to be confirmed by the Game Designer after Build's report. Reopened for Phase 1: the Build found contradictions and gaps in spec sections 3.2–3.4 (handoff 33); step 6b resolved them (handoff 35, D-008). Reopened again after Phase 1 Verify: criteria 1 and 2 (Phase 1 part) cannot be judged from a build description, and QA raised design items D1–D6 (handoff 40); step 7c settled them (handoff 44, D-009: criteria 1 and 2 are judged at the playtest from counts; D2–D6 answered; no rule change). Opened once more after the narrow re-verify (handoff 50): spec 3.3 wording items and a neutral playtest record sheet (step 7h, handoff 52).
- Plan: needed for Phase 2 (feasibility and cost). Answered in handoff 16 and D-004: feasible and cheap. Phase 1 needs no separate Plan (handoff 16).
- Build: the dash-damage defect fix (done, handoff 08), the hit-stun input made observable (done, handoff 20), then Phase 1 (done, handoff 37), a narrow Phase 1 follow-up (ring dot colour, build statements, spec clarification; done, handoff 47), and Phase 2 of the chosen direction.
