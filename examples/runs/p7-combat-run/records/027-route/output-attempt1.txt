# 28: studio → game-designer — Diagnose the combat variety problems a fresh playtester still reports in build 0.5

## Task

Using the playtest report in handoff 27, diagnose why combat still feels monotonous across rooms 1–5. Give causes and solution options in design terms (`diagnose-experience`).

## Context

G-001: the Owner said combat is monotonous. The first Diagnose (04) led to the 0.5 changes. QA passed criteria 1–17 at 30 and 60 FPS. A fresh playtester then played the build through input scripts, with the player view only and no knowledge of what changed. The playtester still found combat monotonous in places. This is the second time the Goal enters Diagnose. Loop limit: a third pass without measurable progress goes to `game-director`.

## Findings

Experience issues from the playtest (27, stated as the playtester's observations):

1. **Melee enemies all draw the same reaction.** Grub, runner and brute each get "sidestep the warning, then swipe or ignore". Rooms 1 and 2 felt interchangeable apart from enemy count. The runner's short warning never tested the playtester. The brute asked for patience, not a different approach.
2. **The dash was never needed against melee.** A one-tile sidestep always sufficed. Dash and sidestep were indistinguishable in room 1.
3. **Spitters were the only enemy that changed how the playtester moved.** Their solution was still "keep moving", and with several spitters the room turned into attrition.
4. **Spitter rooms 3–5 became repetitive or a crossfire the playtester found unreadable.** In rooms 4 and 5 two spitters in opposite corners fire in sync and their lines cross. Stepping aside from one line sometimes put the playtester in another, and the playtester could not tell which line to avoid. They felt this was the least fair moment.
5. **The brute gave no reason to fight it.** The playtester asked whether it is meant to be attacked at all.

Positives to keep: warnings were consistently readable, rooms were short, and cancelling or staggering a lunge with a swipe felt good.

Limits on the evidence:
- All play was scripted. Reaction scripts react in about 0.15 s every time, which is steadier than a human. Human-reaction problems are likely hidden and dodging is probably flattered.
- Not seen: how the room 2 and room 3 sessions ended, a brute kill, room 3 cleared, the practice room, or timed (non-reactive) scripts in rooms 2–5.
- Each reaction script ran once per room, so single outcomes are anecdotes.
- You may use `run_trace` and `run_bots` to check or extend a claim. Say which findings rest on the playtester's report alone.

The playtester's possible defect (grub hit at about 6.0 s after a swipe in room 2) goes to QA as handoff 29. Do not diagnose it here.
